# The One-Page Verdict Template

After every paper you read, fill this in. It takes five to ten minutes, and it
*is* the reading — everything else was just collecting material for it.

Fill it by hand or in your notes app. You only fully own what you can write in your
own words, on one page.

Copy everything between the lines:

---

## Paper Verdict: [one-line nickname so you can find this later]

**Title / first author / year / link or DOI:**
____

### The question
*In one plain sentence, what did the authors set out to find out? (Find it in the
end of the Introduction.)*
____

### What they did
*Study kind (watched vs. assigned?), how long, humans/mice/databases, and where.*
____

### Who was studied
**n = __** · who they were · where · who got left out · resemblance to the person
you're reading for:
____

### The main result, in absolute numbers
*Not "improved!" — the same fact from 3-in-100 → 5-in-100 spelled out. Which figure
or table it came from:*
____

### Strong or weak claim?
*Hedge words found ("may," "suggests," "associated with") vs. sturdy language
(randomized, pre-registered, replicated). One sentence:*
____

### Money & conflicts
*Funding statement · author employers · COI statement · registered? (NCT number) ·
any independent replication:*
____

### The authors' admitted limitations
*Two or three, copied from Discussion in your own words:*
____

### My confidence: High / Medium / Low
*Because:*
____

### Plain-English takeaway
**If a friend asked me what this paper says, I'd tell them:** ____

### What I'll do
☐ Nothing yet — just a note · ☐ Watch for more research · ☐ Ask a professional
about it · ☐ Worth adjusting my approach

### One question I'd ask the authors
____

## How to score yourself (gently)

- If you can't fill "The question" in one sentence: skim the Introduction's *last*
  two paragraphs again — it's nearly always there.
- If "Main result in absolute numbers" stays empty: you've found the paper's soft
  spot, which is a real and useful verdict all by itself.
- If "Money & conflicts" is blank, that's data too — papers that file well make
  disclosure easy to find, so blanks you had to hunt for are worth noticing.
- If your plain-English takeaway comes out longer than two sentences: Pass 2 needs
  a second go — that's not failure, that's your map saying "unfinished."

Verdicts you don't trust enough to keep in a folder usually deserve a second pass.
Then again — a stack of written verdicts is how you develop taste for this field
in a few months instead of a few years.

> **Education only.** A completed verdict is your reading summary, not a diagnosis,
> prescription, or professional opinion. Share it with the right professional before
> acting on anything in health, law, or money.
