# Start Here: Reading Research Papers Like a Friend Would

**A non-student beginner's guide to reading formal papers — no grad school required.**

If you've ever opened a research paper PDF, felt the wall of jargon hit, and quietly
closed the tab — you're in the right place. Research papers aren't written to exclude
you. They're written by people who learned a specific house style, and nobody ever
handed you the decoder ring. This guide is that decoder ring.

You don't need to be a nurse, a student, or a scientist. You just need to know how to
*read* a paper: what the parts are, how to skim on purpose, how to tell a strong claim
from a shaky one, and how to turn dense sentences into plain English.

   of checkboxes.
8. **[Glossary](glossary.md)** — paper-reading words explained plainly, in case you
   meet them out in the wild.

## A friendly promise

Nothing here assumes background knowledge, and nothing here will talk down to you.
"Difficult" in research papers mostly means "unfamiliar," and unfamiliar is fixable.

## A small disclaimer, said with respect

This guide is **for education only**. It isn't medical, legal, or financial advice,
and reading papers with it doesn't make you a doctor, lawyer, or reviewer. It *does*
make you a smarter reader — which is a superpower in its own right. If a paper makes
you want to change something about your health or your care, take that verdict to a
professional who can see the whole picture.

This guide complements **ai-for-nurse-educators-guide** and
**patient-teaching-with-ai-guide**: those explain how to use AI for teaching and
patient education; this one builds the deeper skill underneath both — reading the
actual sources.
