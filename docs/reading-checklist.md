# Quick Reading Checklist

The whole method on one page. Print it, tape it inside a book cover, or keep it in
your notes app. Tick as you go — and if a tick doesn't happen today, that's normal;
papers keep.

## Pass 1 — The Skim (5 min)

- [ ] Title read with attention to its *shape* ("randomized…" vs "association…")
- [ ] Abstract read as a *trailer*, not a verdict
- [ ] Every section heading scanned
- [ ] Figure and table captions glanced at
- [ ] Keep / drop decided on purpose

## Pass 2 — The Map (15–20 min)

- [ ] "The question was: ____" written in one sentence
- [ ] "The authors say they found: ____" written in one sentence
- [ ] Words-I-don't-know list started (don't stop to define yet)
- [ ] One-Page Verdict sheet opened and filled in with the first two fields

## Pass 3 — The Read (30–60 min)

- [ ] Who was studied and how many (n) — from Methods
- [ ] How participants got in, who was left out
- [ ] What was measured, and when
- [ ] Each Discussion claim matched to its number/figure in Results
- [ ] Axes of every key figure actually read
- [ ] Authors' limitations list copied into your verdict
- [ ] Two or three bottleneck references noticed (the same ones cited everywhere)

## Pass 4 — The Verdict (10 min)

- [ ] Main result in absolute numbers, not just percentages
- [ ] Funding statement read
- [ ] Author affiliations scanned for the company-shaped ones
- [ ] COI statement found (or its absence noted)
- [ ] Trial registration number found, if there is one
- [ ] Jargon list translated with the [3-step method](05-jargon-to-plain-english.md)
- [ ] Confidence marked: High / Medium / Low — with one reason
- [ ] Plain-English takeaway written ("I'd tell a friend…")
- [ ] Decision marked: nothing yet / watch / ask a professional / act
- [ ] One question I'd ask the authors

## Sanity habits (any pass)

- [ ] Headline vs. paper compared, if I met a headline
- [ ] "May / suggests / associated with" priced at "early," not "proven"
- [ ] Absolute numbers asked for whenever a percentage showed up
- [ ] "Statistically significant" translated as "probably not luck," not "important"
- [ ] I stopped when tired and came back — the map was still there

## After reading

- [ ] Verdict sheet completed and put somewhere findable
- [ ] New words added to my personal glossary
- [ ] If something in the paper touches my health or someone else's care:
      verdict shared with a professional, not acted on alone

> **Education only.** Checkboxes make you a sharper reader, not a reviewer,
> clinician, or lawyer. The next step for anything that matters is a conversation
> with someone trained for it — and you'll be bringing better questions to that
> conversation than most anyone.
